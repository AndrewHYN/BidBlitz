# BidBlitz launch playbook — Zimbabwe-first, measurable growth

This is a **proposed experiment plan**, not a report of existing traction.
No fabricated stats, paid advertisements, bots, fake countdowns, or unsolicited bulk messaging.

## Positioning

BidBlitz is a local timed-auction marketplace: sellers list genuine goods; buyers place competing bids; close, payment, handover and dispute rules are explicit. Differentiate on *transparent bids, real seller identities, safer handover, and visible transaction status* rather than claiming to be the cheapest marketplace.

## First 30 days: supply before hype

**Week 1 — the seller seed**
- Personal founder outreach to 20-30 sellers in Harare; target gaming accessories, small electronics and verified local merchants with good photos and reasonable starting bids.
- Require truthful condition/defects, clear item pictures, location, handover options, and actual seller ownership.
- Publish simple seller explainer videos: how to list, what counts as a valid bid, what happens after winning, and what BidBlitz's 5% covers.
- Collect explicit permission for any seller story, video or product image reuse.

**Week 2 — the first themed auction**
- Create a staff campaign in **Admin → Marketing → Campaign pipeline** with objective AUCTION_EVENT.
- Use the campaign's unique tracking code for a **Blitz Drop** collection of real live auctions. Theme examples: Gaming Gear Friday, Campus Tech, Small Business Upgrade.
- Promote **real** listing end times; never fabricate countdowns or competing bidders.
- Ask selected sellers to share their auction links via their own accounts. Provide graphics without fake testimonials.

**Week 3 — demand experiments**
- Publish honest short-form product demos on Instagram, WhatsApp Status, Facebook communities (where allowed), and creator-owned channels.
- Give each placement a distinct `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content`.
- Test seller recruitment vs buyer discovery creative, changing only one main variable at a time.
- Ask willing buyers to describe the bidding experience. Verified completed-purchase reviews only.

**Week 4 — reinforce what works**
- Review real outcomes: qualified seller sign-ups, published compliant listings, first bids, paid transactions, completed handovers, disputes and repeat buyers.
- Stop campaigns bringing traffic but no verified listings or completed transactions.
- Reinvest only from **available platform income**, never seller money or unapplied buyer collections.

## Growth studio workflows

1. **Draft:** Add title, channel, goal, destination, unique campaign code, proposed USD budget, date and creative brief.
2. **Ready:** Have the growth manager review wording and item claims before promotion.
3. **Running:** Staff manually mark the campaign active after launching content externally. This does **not** buy an ad, schedule an email, send messages or publish posts automatically.
4. **Paused:** Stop sharing that campaign; retain the audit history.
5. **Completed:** Record that the planned activity ended. Completion is terminal; clone with a new code for the next experiment.

Copy the unique link and use that on the channel. UTM tags are only labels until compatible consent-aware analytics and server attribution are implemented. A shared URL is not evidence of actual clicks or purchases.

## Staff structure and hiring

**Initial (founder + one part-time growth operator)**: founder handles finance, platform safety and approved promotions; growth operator handles seller recruitment, content, community engagement and campaigns. Assign MARKETING role; it grants campaign management but **no financial permissions**. Finance analyst, support agent, listing reviewer and dispute agent can be shared/part-time until volume justifies distinct hires, with conflicts of interest and access separation for money decisions.

**First hire practical exercise:** give them one real listing, ask for a 20-second video outline, two captions in English and Shona, a creator outreach message, one safe campaign plan with UTM tags, and one risk they would report rather than promote. Evaluate clarity, speed, originality and accuracy; avoid claims about unproven payouts.

## Measurement board (targets, not claims)

| Metric | First target | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified seed sellers | 20-30 | Enough good inventory to browse |
| Compliant active auctions | 30-50 | Supply depth |
| Unique creator placements | 6-10 | Test low-cost acquisition routes |
| Listing-to-first-bid rate | Measure baseline first | Auction engagement |
| Paid sale conversion | Measure baseline first | Proves payment journey |
| Disputes per 100 completed sales | Track each case | Trust |
| Net platform contribution per completed sale | 5% fee minus provider fees and actual acquisition spend | Avoid unprofitable growth |
| Repeat buyers within 30 days | Track from real purchases | Retention |

Do not claim automatic campaign ROI until attribution is implemented. Saved campaign budget is *planned*, not spent; the application never debits an account.

## Unique formats to test (not deceptive mechanics)

- **Blitz Drops:** seller-curated genuine auctions with a real weekly theme.
- **Listing Glow-Up:** show a seller's before/after photography (with permission); teach clear condition disclosures.
- **Campus Gear Exchange:** support student-oriented gaming and accessories with verified seller details and safer daytime meetup guidance.
- **How the Blitz Works:** 15-second explainer clips showing max-bid rules, anti-sniping, and when payment is due.
- **Creator vs Seller Challenge:** creators review *legitimate* listings; no staged bids.
- **Buyer Safety Minute:** quick tips on public meeting spots, checking goods and avoiding advance/off-platform payment scams.

## Spending discipline

Before approving **any** paid campaign, compare the platform's projected 5% fee on genuine settled transactions with the provider payment/payout charges and expected acquisition cost. A $20 campaign requires $400 in genuine, fee-bearing gross transaction volume **before payment costs** just to cover the ad budget. That illustration is not a revenue forecast; it ignores costs and customer behavior.

The next implementation items are consent-aware event attribution, referrals with anti-abuse, campaign budget actual-vs-planned accounting, and CRM opt-in/unsubscribe tracking. Until then, the planner intentionally makes no performance claims.
